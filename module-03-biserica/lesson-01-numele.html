God, Lord, Spirit: the names

The words a sermon says most often, and the grammar of addressing God.

25 minNo audio neededVocabulary

If you could only learn twenty words before Sunday, these would be most of them.

The names

Guess, then tap

Most carry the glued-on “the”: Domn + -ul, Duh + -ul, Fiu + -l. Dumnezeu never takes it, and uses lui for “of”: Fiul lui Dumnezeu, the Son of God.

Capital letters

Cornilescu capitalises pronouns that refer to God. In John 1 you will see El and Lui; in the prayer, Tău and Ta. On a slide the capital tells you who is meant.

Calling on Him

Romanian has a special form for addressing someone directly. For these names it usually ends in -e:

NameWhen callingMeaning
DomnDoamne!Lord!
IsusIsuse!Jesus!
TatăTată!Father!
DumnezeuDumnezeule!O God!

You have already read one in Luke 15: the son says Tată, am păcătuit.

Listen for Doamne. Public prayer in Romanian is full of it. Every time you hear it, a new sentence is starting.

Domnul is the Lord. domnul with a small d is “mister”, as in domnul Popescu. The capital letter is carrying a great deal of theology.
