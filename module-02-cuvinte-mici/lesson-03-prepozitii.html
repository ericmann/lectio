In, on, with, from: the prepositions

A dozen short words that say where, when and with whom.

25 minNo audio neededDrill inside

These are among the most frequent words in any Romanian text. One corpus count puts de, în, la, cu, pe and din at the top.

The core twelve

WordMeaningFrom Scripture
îninîn pășuni verzi, in green pastures
peonpe cărări drepte, on right paths
laat, tola ape de odihnă, to waters of rest
cuwithcu mine, with me
deof, fromplin de har, full of grace
dinfrom, out ofdin pricina, because of
pentruforpentru copii, for children
printhroughprin valea, through the valley
fărăwithoutfără El, without Him
dupăafter, according toEvanghelia după Matei
pânăuntilpână la sfârșitul, until the end
despreaboutdespre dragoste, about love

The list follows a standard teaching set. The examples come from Psalm 23 and John 1.

Nothing changes after them. The everyday prepositions take the plain form of the noun, so there is no new ending to learn here.

Two things English doesn't do

  • pe also marks a person as the object of a verb: vor vedea pe Dumnezeu, they will see God. It isn't translated.
  • Before un and o, three of them fuse: într-un, dintr-un, printr-un.

de means of, from, by, about and since. If you can't work out what a short word is doing, it is probably de.
