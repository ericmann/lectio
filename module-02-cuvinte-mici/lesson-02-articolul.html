The article that sticks to the end of the noun

Romanian has no separate word for “the”. Once you can peel it off the end of a noun, half of every Bible verse opens up.

25 minNo audio neededDrill inside

You have met this already: Tatăl, Numele, Împărăția. Romanian fastens “the” onto the end of the noun, and one guide calls this the make-or-break feature of the early language.

“A” goes in front, “the” goes behind

“A” works like English: un for masculine and neuter, o for feminine. “The” is a suffix, and its shape depends on how the noun ends.

Noun ends inAddExample
a consonant-uldomn → domnul, the lord
-u-lfiu → fiul, the son
-e (masculine)-lefrate → fratele, the brother
-ă (feminine)-ă becomes -acasă → casa, the house
-e (feminine)-apâine → pâinea, the bread

The masculine rules are fully predictable, the feminine swap is -ă to -a, and -e plus -a fuses into -ea.

Plurals

Masculine plurals in -i double it: frați → frații, the brothers. Plurals in -e add -le: zile → zilele, the days.

“Of the”: the endings sermons are full of

A second set of endings means “of the” or “to the”. One summary lists them as -lui for a masculine owner and -ei for a feminine one; the plural is -lor.

Guess, then tap

Names don't take the ending. They put lui in front instead: Cuvântul lui Dumnezeu, the Word of God.

English says “the end of the days of the life of me”. Romanian says sfârșitul zilelor vieții mele and gets on with its morning.
