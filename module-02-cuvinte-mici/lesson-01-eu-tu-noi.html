I, you, we: pronouns, a fi and a avea

Eight pronouns and two verbs. Between them they appear in almost every sentence you will hear.

25 minNo audio neededDrill inside

Week one taught you to say the words. This week you learn the small ones that hold a sentence together, starting with who is doing what.

The eight pronouns

RomanianEnglishNotes
euIsaid “yeoo”
tuyouone person, familiar. Also how God is addressed: Tu
el · eahe · she“yel” and “ya”
noiwe
voiyou allwhat a preacher calls the congregation
ei · eletheymen or mixed · women

Mostly you won't see them. Romanian drops the pronoun because the verb ending already says who. Sunt aici is a full sentence: “I am here.”

a fi, to be

FormMeaningFormMeaning
suntI amsuntemwe are
eștiyou aresuntețiyou all are
este or ehe, she, it issuntthey are

These are the present forms of a fi. Notice that sunt does double duty for “I am” and “they are”; context decides.

a avea, to have

FormMeaningFormMeaning
amI haveavemwe have
aiyou haveavețiyou all have
arehe, she, it hasauthey have

Hold on to these six. In week four the short ones come back as the helpers that build the past tense.

To say “not”, put nu in front. Nu am is “I don't have”. Romanian toddlers master this construction first.
