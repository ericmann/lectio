Numbers, days, and telling when

Enough numbers to find a chapter and verse, and enough calendar to read a schedule.

25 minNo audio neededDrill inside

When a preacher says “open to…”, the next three seconds are all numbers. Today is for those three seconds.

One to ten

Guess, then tap

Bigger ones

  • Teens end in -sprezece: doisprezece is 12, as in cei doisprezece, the twelve. șaisprezece is 16.
  • Tens end in -zeci: douăzeci 20, treizeci 30, patruzeci 40.
  • Join with și: douăzeci și trei is 23.
  • o sută is 100, o mie is 1,000.

How a reference is spoken. In the author's experience, chapter and verse are joined with cu: Ioan trei cu șaisprezece is John 3:16. In print you will see capitolul 3, versetul 16.

First, second, third

Sermon points are counted with în primul rând (first), în al doilea rând (second), în al treilea rând (third).

Days are written in lowercase and need no word for “on”. A phrasebook correction makes the same point: Ne vedem luni, with nothing in front, is “See you Monday”.

Telling when

Clock times take la: la ora cinci, at five o'clock. Add dimineața (in the morning) or seara (in the evening). azi or astăzi is today, mâine tomorrow, ieri yesterday, acum now.

nouă is nine. It is also “new” and “to us”. Romanian is thrifty with spellings.
