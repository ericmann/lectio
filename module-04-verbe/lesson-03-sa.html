Să: the word sermons run on

One two-letter word covers “to”, “let us”, “may” and “so that”.

25 minNo audio neededDrill inside

If week four had to be one lesson, it would be this one. Să is in every prayer, every invitation and every application point.

What it is

Să plus a verb makes the subjunctive. It is used after verbs like want, can and must, and for purpose, and Romanian leans on it more than its sister languages do.

Four jobs

JobExampleMeaning
“to” after another verbvreau să citescI want to read
“let us”să ne rugămlet us pray
“may”Dumnezeu să vă binecuvântezemay God bless you
“so that”, with capentru ca … să aibă viațaso that … may have life

Three verbs trigger it constantly: vreau (I want), pot (I can), trebuie (must). After any of them, expect să.

The form changes for “he” and “they”

For I, you, we and you all, the verb after să looks like the ordinary present. For he, she and they, the last vowel flips:

PresentAfter săVerb
facesă facăto do
vinesă vinăto come
aresă aibăto have
estesă fieto be

The regular flips appear in the table for a face; the two irregular ones are să aibă and să fie.

You met this form on day one. Sfințească-se, vie and facă-se in the prayer are the same flipped forms, used without să in an older, solemn style.

English needs “to”, “let's”, “may” and “so that”. Romanian has să and a free afternoon.
