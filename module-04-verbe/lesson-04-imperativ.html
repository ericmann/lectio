Commands and requests

How Romanian tells one person, or a whole room, to do something.

25 minNo audio neededDrill inside

Preaching ends in imperatives: come, listen, believe, go. They are short, and they are said with feeling, which makes them easy to catch.

Only two people can be commanded

The imperative exists only for the second person: tu, one person, and voi, a group.

To a group

This is the form a preacher uses, and it is the ordinary “you all” form: veniți (come), ascultați (listen), citiți (read), faceți (do).

To one person

Usually it matches the “he, she” form: citește, read; spune, say. A few very common verbs have short forms of their own:

Guess, then tap

The forms fă, dă and fii are listed for a face, a da and a fi.

Pronouns attach behind

With a command the little pronoun moves to the end, on a hyphen. The prayer is full of these: dă-ne (give us), iartă-ne (forgive us), izbăvește-ne (deliver us). Another you will hear: spune-mi, tell me.

Saying “don't”

  • To a group: nu plus the same form. Nu vă temeți, do not be afraid.
  • To one person: nu plus the dictionary form. nu ne duce în ispită, do not lead us into temptation.

Softening it. te rog (to one) and vă rog (to several, or politely) mean “please”. Literally: “I ask you”.

Vino! is “come!” to one person. Veniți! is “come!” to everyone. Shouting the first at a congregation gets you exactly one confused deacon.
