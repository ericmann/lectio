The past with am, ai, a

One helper and one ending give you nearly every past action in the Bible.

25 minNo audio neededDrill inside

Stories are told in the past, and the Gospels are stories. Romanian's everyday past is built from two pieces you half know already.

Helper plus participle

The perfect compus uses a short form of “have” plus a past participle. It is the past tense the spoken language relies on.

WhoHelperExampleMeaning
Iamam cititI read, I have read
youaiai citityou read
he, sheaa citithe read
weamam cititwe read
you allațiați citityou all read
theyauau cititthey read

The helpers are the ones listed for this tense. Three of them, am, ai and au, are the forms of a avea from lesson 2.1. The others are shortened versions.

Spotting the participle

Most end in -t: iubit (loved), dat (given), făcut (done, made), venit (come), văzut (seen). A few end in -s: zis (said), scris (written).

Squeezed forms

Short words in front of the helper fuse with it, joined by a hyphen:

  • nu + a → n-a: n-a biruit-o, has not overcome it.
  • se + a → s-a: S-a făcut trup, became flesh.
  • îl + a → l-a: l-a văzut, saw him.

The other “was”. era is an ongoing past: was, used to be. a fost is a finished one. John 1:1 uses era four times for good reason.

am means “I have”, “we have”, and with a participle “I did”. Romanian found a useful word and declined to make a second one.
