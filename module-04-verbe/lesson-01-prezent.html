The present tense and its four families

How to tell who is acting from the last two letters of a verb.

25 minNo audio neededDrill inside

Nouns name things. Verbs are where a sentence happens, and Romanian verbs say who, when and sometimes why in a single word.

Four families

Every verb belongs to one of four classes, set by the ending of its dictionary form:

Ends inExamples
-aa cânta to sing · a ierta to forgive
-eaa vedea to see · a avea to have
-ea face to do · a crede to believe
-i or -îa veni to come · a citi to read

The a in front is the “to” of the dictionary form. One present tense covers both “I work” and “I am working”.

The forms are from the tables for a face and a citi.

Two endings never lie. -m is always “we” and -ți is always “you all”. In the prayer, iertăm is “we forgive”.

The little word in front

Many verbs carry a pronoun that turns the action back on the doer: mă (myself), te, se, ne, vă. se bucură is “rejoices”, mă tem is “I am afraid”, ne rugăm is “we pray”.

Romanian doesn't pray. It “asks itself”, a se ruga. Grammar books call this reflexive. Theologians could do a lot with it.
